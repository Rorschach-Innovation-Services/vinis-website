Case study 02 · Tailings safety

Watching a tailings dam — every way at once.

Tailings storage facilities are among the highest-consequence structures in mining — yet the person responsible day-to-day is usually a SHEQ manager, not a geotechnical engineer. VINIS combines satellite, drone, sensors, manual inspection and AI to make monitoring happen on schedule, capture defensible evidence, and keep the facility compliant and safe.

01 The challenge

Well-monitored dams still fail — for human reasons.

When a monitored tailings dam fails, the causes are usually organisational, not technical: risks that never reach the people who can act, concerns overridden by cost, monitoring not aligned to how the dam actually fails, and thin specialist capacity. The tools exist — operationalising them against a compliance obligation is the gap.

01

Too few qualified inspectors

Geotechnical specialists are scarce and expensive. Routine oversight falls to SHEQ managers who aren't engineers — and can't be everywhere.

02

Manual inspection is subjective

Two inspectors rarely grade the same wall the same way, and paper records can be edited after the fact — so the evidence isn't defensible.

03

Infrequent, inconsistent

Periodic audits leave long blind windows. Overtopping — the single biggest cause of failure — can develop between them.

02 The approach

One dam, five streams.

No single method sees everything. VINIS fuses five independent ways of watching the dam — each covering what the others miss — into one calibrated, cross-validated evidence base.

Satellite

Sentinel-2 every ~5 days measures pond area, beach width and freeboard — and flags undeclared wall raises and rising overtopping risk from orbit.

Drone

Cm-level photogrammetry — orthomosaics and DEMs — closes the gap between 10 m pixels and the ground: beach distance, erosion, crest and wall survey, without walking the deposit.

Sensors

Continuous ground-instrument telemetry — pore pressure, movement, seepage — as a live time-series, positioned on the facility record.

Manual inspection

Structured, geotagged, time-stamped rounds on the inspector's own phone — progressive forms keyed to the standard checklist, with a tamper-proof chain of custody.

AI

Machine-vision screening and cross-validation — one stream's finding checked against the others (a drone DEM confirming the satellite pond edge), with an uncertainty band on every number.

03 What it changes

Compliance you can defend — and safety you can trust.

Beyond the inspector shortage

The routine runs without a geotechnical engineer on site — remote streams do the watching, and specialists are pulled in by exception, escalated in one tap to the named Engineer of Record.

Objective, not subjective

Every observation is a standardised, structured record against the same checklist and site-calibrated thresholds — with an uncertainty band. No more two-inspectors-two-answers.

Higher frequency

Satellite every ~5 days, scheduled drone flights, continuous sensors and prompted inspection rounds replace the occasional audit — the blind windows close.

Consistent, reportable data

One standardised evidence base means the numbers that go into a report are derived the same way every time, at every site — comparable and audit-ready.

04 Compliance & evidence

The audit trail writes the report.

Every observation, alert and acknowledgement is an immutable, hash-chained event. Compliance reports are generated from that ledger — reproducible and tamper-evident by construction.

Append-only evidence

Nothing can be edited or deleted after the fact — the record is defensible under audit.

Automated reports

Daily brief to quarterly GISTM disclosure packs, assembled from the ledger — never hand-compiled.

Live conformance scorecard

Mapped topic-by-topic to exactly what the platform can evidence, so gaps are visible.
